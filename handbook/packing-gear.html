Packing Lists & Gear

Packing Lists & Gear

Proper packing and gear selection are essential for safe, comfortable, and successful outdoor adventures. This section provides comprehensive packing guidance for various activities.

The Ten Essentials

Any time a scout goes camping or hiking, she should always carry these essential items for emergency preparedness and outdoor safety.

Essential Items Checklist

  • Pocketknife (when you complete Totin’ Chip training)
  • First Aid Kit (personal kit with basic supplies)
  • Extra clothing (appropriate for weather conditions)
  • Rain gear (poncho or rain jacket and pants)
  • Filled water bottle (minimum 1 liter capacity)
  • Flashlight or headlamp (with extra batteries)
  • Trail food (energy bars, nuts, dried fruit)
  • Matches/fire starter (when you complete Firem’n Chit training)
  • Sun protection (hat, sunscreen, sunglasses)
  • Map and compass (appropriate for activity area)

Recommended Additional Items

  • Insect repellant (appropriate for season and location)
  • Safety whistle (for emergency signaling)
  • Toilet paper (small roll in plastic bag with hand sanitizer)

Tent Camping Checklist

For typical weekend tent camping trips, use this comprehensive packing list.

Essential Gear

  • Ten Essentials (all items listed above)
  • Sleeping bag (appropriate temperature rating)
  • Sleeping pad (for insulation and comfort)
  • Pillow (optional - can use inflatable or stuff sack)

Clothing

  • Scout uniform (wear to and from campsite)
  • Weather-appropriate clothes (layers recommended)
  • Pajamas/sleepwear
  • Extra underwear and socks
  • Boots or hiking shoes
  • Camp shoes (comfortable shoes for around camp)

Personal Items

  • Toiletries (toothbrush, toothpaste, soap, etc.)
  • Towel (quick-dry camping towel preferred)
  • Personal medications (in original containers)

Dining and Activities

  • Mess kit (fork/spoon/knife, plate, cup in mesh bag)
  • Scout handbook (for advancement and reference)
  • Small backpack/daypack (for day activities)
  • Books for reading (entertainment during downtime)

Practical Items

  • Trash bag (for personal trash and wet items)
  • Plastic bags (for organizing and waterproofing)
  • Duct tape (small roll for repairs)

Seasonal Considerations

Summer Camping

Additional items for hot weather:

  • Extra water bottles or hydration system
  • Lightweight, breathable clothing
  • Sun hat with wide brim
  • Higher SPF sunscreen
  • Cooling towel
  • Insect repellant (especially important)

Winter Camping

Additional items for cold weather:

  • Warmer sleeping bag (rated for expected temperatures)
  • Insulated sleeping pad
  • Thermal underwear and layers
  • Warm hat and gloves
  • Waterproof boots
  • Hand and foot warmers
  • Extra high-energy food

Rainy Season

Additional items for wet conditions:

  • Extra rain gear and waterproof layers
  • Waterproof bag for electronics
  • Extra socks and underwear
  • Quick-dry clothing materials
  • Waterproof matches or lighter
  • Tarp for additional shelter

Personal Gear vs. Troop Equipment

Provided by Troop

  • Tents and rain flies
  • Cooking equipment (stoves, pots, utensils)
  • Patrol boxes with basic supplies
  • First aid kits (patrol-level)
  • Water containers (large patrol containers)

Personal Responsibility

  • All clothing and personal items
  • Individual sleeping systems
  • Personal first aid supplies
  • Individual water bottles
  • Personal tools and equipment
  • Entertainment and reading materials

Packing Tips

Organization Strategies

  • Use stuff sacks or packing cubes to organize gear
  • Keep similar items together (all clothes, all toiletries)
  • Pack items you’ll need first on top
  • Keep rain gear easily accessible

Weight Management

  • Pack only what you need - avoid overpacking
  • Share weight with patrol members when possible
  • Choose lightweight, multi-purpose items
  • Consider leaving cotton clothes at home (poor performance when wet)

Waterproofing

  • Use trash bags or dry bags for clothes and sleeping gear
  • Keep electronics in waterproof containers
  • Pack extra bags for wet or dirty items
  • Separate clean and dirty clothes

Gear Maintenance

Before the Trip

  • Check all equipment for damage or wear
  • Test electronics and replace batteries
  • Clean and prepare gear as needed
  • Make repairs before heading out

During the Trip

  • Keep gear organized and in designated places
  • Protect gear from weather and rough handling
  • Report damaged troop gear immediately
  • Take care of personal equipment

After the Trip

  • Clean and dry all gear before storing
  • Make needed repairs promptly
  • Replace consumable items (batteries, first aid supplies)
  • Store gear properly for next use

Special Activity Gear

Day Hiking

  • Lighter pack with essentials only
  • Extra water and snacks
  • Appropriate footwear
  • Map and compass for route

Water Activities

  • Quick-dry clothing
  • Water shoes
  • Sunscreen (waterproof)
  • Extra dry clothes in waterproof bag

Service Projects

  • Work clothes that can get dirty
  • Work gloves
  • Safety equipment as required
  • Tools (if personally owned and appropriate)

Budget-Friendly Gear Tips

Cost-Saving Strategies

  • Start with basic, functional items rather than premium gear
  • Look for sales and discounts at outdoor retailers
  • Consider used gear from other scouts or outdoor gear swaps
  • Ask for gear as gifts for birthdays and holidays
  • Share or borrow specialized items until you know what you prefer

Investment Priorities

  1. Good sleeping bag - essential for comfort and safety
  2. Quality rain gear - keeps you dry and comfortable
  3. Proper footwear - prevents injuries and improves enjoyment
  4. Reliable flashlight/headlamp - critical for safety

Winter Camping Gear List

Winter camping is one of the most rewarding experiences in Scouting — but it demands extra preparation and the right gear. Cold weather, snow, and shorter daylight hours mean your equipment choices directly affect your safety and comfort.

Key principle: Cotton kills. In winter conditions, wet cotton clothing draws heat from your body rapidly. Choose wool, fleece, and synthetic fabrics for all layers.

All items from the Ten Essentials and Tent Camping Checklist are still required. Winter additions are on top of standard gear.

The Layering System

Effective winter camping depends on a proper layering system. Each layer serves a specific purpose:

Base Layer (Moisture Management)

  • Moisture-wicking long underwear top (synthetic or merino wool — NO cotton)
  • Moisture-wicking long underwear bottoms (synthetic or merino wool — NO cotton)
  • Liner socks (thin synthetic or silk)

Mid Layer (Insulation)

  • Fleece or wool jacket/pullover
  • Insulated pants (fleece-lined or soft shell)
  • Wool or fleece vest (optional extra core warmth)

Outer Layer (Wind & Weather Protection)

  • Waterproof/windproof jacket (with hood)
  • Waterproof/windproof pants (snow pants or shell pants)
  • Gaiters (keeps snow out of boots)

Winter Sleeping System

  • Cold-weather sleeping bag (rated at least 10°F below expected low temperature)
  • Insulated sleeping pad (R-value 5.0 or higher recommended)
  • Second sleeping pad (closed-cell foam pad under inflatable for extra insulation)
  • Sleeping bag liner (adds 10–15°F of warmth)
  • Stuff sack pillow (filled with next day’s clothes)

Winter Footwear

  • Insulated, waterproof winter boots (rated for expected temperatures)
  • Wool or synthetic hiking socks (2–3 pairs, NO cotton)
  • Camp booties or extra warm socks (for inside tent/sleeping)
  • Boot liners (if boots are removable-liner type, bring extras)

Head, Hands & Extremities

  • Warm winter hat (wool or fleece, covers ears)
  • Balaclava or neck gaiter (face and neck protection)
  • Insulated gloves (waterproof outer recommended)
  • Glove liners (thin layer for dexterity)
  • Mittens (warmer than gloves for extreme cold)
  • Chemical hand warmers (several pairs)
  • Chemical toe warmers (several pairs)

Winter-Specific Gear

  • Insulated water bottle carrier (prevents freezing)
  • Wide-mouth water bottles (narrow mouths freeze shut)
  • Thermos (for hot drinks and soup throughout the day)
  • Headlamp with lithium batteries (cold drains alkaline batteries faster)
  • Extra batteries (keep warm in jacket pocket)
  • Snow shovel (lightweight, for tent platform and campsite)
  • Emergency space blanket
  • Fire starting supplies (waterproof matches AND lighter)
  • Lip balm with SPF (cold air and sun reflection cause chapping)
  • Sunscreen (snow reflects UV rays — sunburn is a real winter risk)
  • Sunglasses (snow glare protection)

Winter Clothing Extras

  • Extra base layer set (in case primary gets wet)
  • Extra socks (more than you think — at least 1 pair per day)
  • Down or synthetic puffy jacket (for camp wear, keep dry)
  • Dry sleeping clothes (dedicated set that never leaves the tent)
  • Bandana or small towel (for wiping moisture)

Winter Camping Tips

Staying Warm

  • Eat a snack before bed — your body generates heat by digesting food
  • Do light exercises before getting in your bag to warm up
  • Sleep with tomorrow’s base layer inside your sleeping bag so it’s warm in the morning
  • Keep water bottles inverted (ice forms at the top first, so the opening stays clear)
  • Never hold it — get up and use the latrine; your body wastes energy keeping urine warm

Avoiding Moisture

  • Vent your tent — condensation from breathing will soak your gear
  • Brush off snow before entering the tent
  • Don’t cook inside the tent — moisture and carbon monoxide risk
  • Change out of damp clothes immediately

Safety

  • Know the signs of hypothermia and frostbite
  • Use the buddy system — check on each other frequently
  • Tell someone if you’re cold — it’s not a weakness, it’s a safety issue
  • Stay hydrated — dehydration is just as dangerous in winter as in summer

High Adventure Gear Lists

High adventure trips go beyond regular troop camping. These programs push scouts physically and mentally, and they require specialized gear tailored to each destination.

Important: Each high adventure base publishes its own official gear list. The lists below are based on published requirements and past troop experience, but always check the official source for the most current requirements before your trip.

National Jamboree — Summit Bechtel Reserve

Location: Mount Hope, West Virginia — Appalachian Mountains at the Summit Bechtel Reserve

What it is: Scouting’s flagship 10-day gathering (July 22–31, 2026) with tens of thousands of Scouts. Stadium shows, adventure sports, pioneer village, Mount Jack hikes, and more across 10,000 acres. This is not a typical campout — it’s a full immersion experience.

What’s provided: Basecamp tents and cots, meals, water stations, and a 22L ALPS Mountaineering “Elevate” day pack with participant neckerchief and patches (all included with registration).

Key gear focus: Broken-in hiking shoes (you’ll walk 5–10+ miles per day), lightweight sleeping bag (40°F–50°F), rain jacket (afternoon thunderstorms are common), 2+ liters of water capacity, sun protection, and 10 days of clothing. Pack light — you carry everything from the bus to your campsite. Patch trading supplies are a Jamboree tradition.

Official site & gear list: jamboree.scouting.org

Philmont Scout Ranch

Location: Cimarron, New Mexico — Sangre de Cristo Mountains (6,500–12,441 ft elevation)

What it is: A 7–12 day backpacking trek covering 50–100+ miles through high desert, mountain forests, and alpine terrain. Everything you need is carried on your back.

Key gear focus: Lightweight backpacking pack (60–75L), 20°F sleeping bag, broken-in hiking boots with ankle support, rain gear, layers for altitude temperature swings, and 3+ liters of water capacity. Every ounce counts.

Official gear list & info: philmontscoutranch.org

Florida Sea Base

Location: Islamorada, Florida Keys — tropical ocean environment

What it is: Ocean-based adventures (sailing, snorkeling, SCUBA, island hopping) lasting 6–8 days in the Florida Keys and Bahamas.

Key gear focus: Quick-dry clothing, reef-safe sunscreen (mandatory), water shoes, UV protection for full days on the water, and waterproof bags for everything. Swim test required.

Official gear list & info: bsaseabase.org

Northern Tier

Location: Ely, Minnesota / Atikokan, Ontario — Boundary Waters Canoe Area and Quetico Provincial Park

What it is: A 6–10 day wilderness canoe trek through remote lakes and rivers, with portages (carrying canoe and gear overland between lakes).

Key gear focus: Waterproof everything (gear goes in Duluth packs — provided by Northern Tier), serious insect protection (head net and DEET are not optional), rain gear, quick-dry clothing, and sturdy water shoes for rugged portage trails. Passport required if crossing into Canada.

Official gear list & info: ntier.org

General High Adventure Preparation

Regardless of which program you attend, these apply to all high adventure trips:

  • Start training at least 6 months before your trip
  • All participants must pass the BSA physical (Parts A, B, and C of the Annual Health and Medical Record)
  • Check the official gear list for your specific program and year — requirements change
  • Don’t buy everything new — borrow or rent specialized gear when possible
  • Test all gear before the trip — never use untested equipment on a high adventure
  • Label everything — gear gets mixed up in group settings

Proper preparation and packing make outdoor adventures safer and more enjoyable for everyone.
