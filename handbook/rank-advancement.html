Advancement Guide

Advancement Guide

Scouting America advancement provides a progressive series of challenges that help Scouts develop skills, confidence, and leadership. Scouts can work on any requirement for advancement or merit badges at any time, but can only advance a rank when ALL requirements for that rank are completed. Rank advancement is earned — it is not automatic.

Advancement to Scout

Welcome to your Scouting adventure! Your Scout rank is your introduction to Scouting and the foundation for everything that follows. This rank should be completed within your first month and teaches you the fundamentals you need to participate fully in troop activities.

Your First 30 Days: What to Expect

Starting Scouting is an exciting adventure! Here’s what you can expect during your first month as you work toward earning your Scout rank.

Your First Meeting Experience

When you walk into your first troop meeting, you’ll be welcomed by your new Scout family:

Meeting Your Patrol

  • You’ll be assigned to a patrol - your small group of fellow Scouts
  • Your patrol leader (an experienced Scout) will help guide you through your first meetings
  • Patrol members will become some of your closest friends in Scouting
  • You’ll participate in patrol activities, games, and skill-building exercises

Learning the Basics

  • Practice the Scout Oath, Law, Motto, and Slogan
  • Learn the Scout sign, salute, and handshake
  • Understand how the patrol method works
  • Get familiar with your Scout handbook
  • Start working on basic knots and skills

Scout Rank Requirements

Your Scout rank covers the essential knowledge every Scout needs. Here’s what you’ll learn and demonstrate:

Scouting Knowledge

  • Scout Oath, Law, Motto, and Slogan: Learn these by heart and explain what they mean to you
  • Scout sign, salute, and handshake: Understand when and how to use each one properly
  • Patrol method: Learn how Scouts work together in small groups
  • Scout advancement: Understand how you progress through ranks and earn merit badges
  • Scout uniform: Know how to wear your uniform properly with pride

Basic Skills

  • Knots: Show how to tie a square knot and two half-hitches - your first practical outdoor skills
  • Pocketknife safety: Demonstrate safe handling and the “blood circle” safety rule
  • Citizenship: Explain the Pledge of Allegiance and tell about the American flag
  • Cyber safety: Complete online training to learn safe internet practices

Scout Participation

  • Scoutmaster conference: A friendly one-on-one chat with your Scoutmaster about what you’ve learned
  • Board of review: Meet with adult leaders to share your Scout knowledge and experience

Requirements Process: How Advancement Works

  • Initially, Scouts get their rank requirements signed off by more advanced Scouts
  • Scouts should bring their Scout Handbook to meetings and outings for sign-offs
  • Scout’s name MUST be inside and outside their book
  • Scouts need a pencil or pen and small notebook for taking notes and tracking progress
  • Make photocopies or take pictures of completed pages every 2-3 months in case the book is lost
  • Unlike in Cub Scouts, parents may not sign off on requirements

Important: Your Scout Handbook is your advancement record - take good care of it and keep it with you at meetings and activities!

Your Scout Family

During your first month, you’ll meet the people who will support your Scouting journey:

Your Patrol Leader

  • An experienced Scout (usually 14-16 years old) who leads your patrol
  • Your primary mentor and guide during meetings and activities
  • Available to answer questions and help you learn Scout skills
  • Will work with you individually to help you complete Scout rank requirements

Your Scoutmaster

  • The adult leader who oversees the troop and your advancement
  • Will meet with you for your Scout rank Scoutmaster conference
  • Available to discuss your goals and help you succeed in Scouting
  • Responsible for ensuring you have a positive and safe Scouting experience

Merit Badge Counselors

  • Adult experts who will help you explore your interests through merit badges
  • Each counselor specializes in different subject areas
  • You’ll work with them to complete merit badge requirements at your own pace

Your Scout Buddy

  • An experienced Scout assigned to help you during your first few months
  • Someone to sit with during meetings and help you feel included
  • Available to answer questions about troop procedures and activities

What You’ll Accomplish

By the end of your first 30 days, you’ll have:

Knowledge and Skills

  • Memorized the Scout Oath, Law, Motto, and Slogan
  • Learned basic Scout procedures and uniform standards
  • Mastered your first outdoor skills (knots and knife safety)
  • Completed cyber safety training
  • Understood how advancement works in Scouting

Achievement and Recognition

  • Earned your Scout rank - your first official milestone!
  • Received your Scout rank patch to wear on your uniform
  • Completed your first Scoutmaster conference and board of review
  • Set goals for your continued advancement toward Tenderfoot rank

Advancement to First Class

Welcome to your journey from Scout to First Class! This is where you’ll learn the essential skills that will serve you throughout your entire Scouting adventure.

The advancement path is outlined in the Scout Handbook. Scouts can work on any requirement for Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class or First Class (ST21) or merit badges at any time. However, they can only advance a rank when ALL requirements for that rank and lower are completed.

Your Journey: Scout to First Class

The ranks Scout → Tenderfoot → Second Class → First Class (ST21) teach you all the fundamental skills you’ll need on your Scouting journey. While we encourage Scouts to work toward First Class within their first 18 months, this is a goal, not a requirement. Every Scout progresses at their own pace, and that’s perfectly okay!

Skills You’ll Master

Outdoor Skills

  • Camping and outdoor ethics (Leave No Trace principles)
  • Cooking skills and camp safety
  • First aid and emergency preparedness
  • Navigation using map and compass
  • Knots and rope work
  • Fire building and outdoor cooking
  • Weather awareness and preparation

Physical Fitness

  • Personal fitness planning and tracking
  • Swimming and water safety
  • Hiking and backpacking basics

Leadership and Citizenship

  • Leadership roles within your patrol
  • Community service projects
  • Citizenship and civic responsibility
  • Communication and teamwork skills

Why First Class matters: Scouts who earn First Class early in their Scouting journey are much more likely to stay in Scouting and eventually earn their Eagle Scout rank. These skills give you the confidence and ability to participate fully in all our outdoor adventures and leadership opportunities.

Timeline and Requirements Overview

  • Scout: Your introduction to Scouting (first 30 days)
  • Tenderfoot: Basic outdoor skills and Scout knowledge (typically 2-4 months after Scout)
  • Second Class: Intermediate camping, cooking, and outdoor skills (typically 6-8 months after Scout)
  • First Class: Advanced outdoor skills, leadership, and citizenship (typically 12-18 months after Scout)

Note: These are aspirational timelines. Every Scout progresses at their own pace based on their participation, effort, and opportunities to complete requirements.

Your Scouting Experience

Monthly Adventures: Campouts and Hiking

Every month, our troop goes on outdoor adventures designed to help you practice and advance your skills:

What to Expect

  • Learn and practice outdoor skills in real situations
  • Work on advancement requirements during activities
  • Build friendships with your patrol members
  • Gain confidence in outdoor settings
  • Have fun while learning!

Your Role

  • Come prepared with the right gear and attitude
  • Participate actively in patrol activities
  • Help with camp setup, cooking, and cleanup
  • Practice leadership when given opportunities
  • Support your fellow Scouts

Summer Camp: Your Big Adventure

Summer camp is a week-long experience that accelerates your advancement and skill development:

What You’ll Do

  • Take merit badge classes in subjects that interest you
  • Participate in camp-wide activities and competitions
  • Practice outdoor skills in a focused environment
  • Meet Scouts from other troops
  • Experience more independence and responsibility

Service Projects: Giving Back

Service is a core part of Scouting, and you’ll have many opportunities to help others through troop projects, community service, supporting Eagle Scout projects, environmental conservation, and helping with troop activities.

Leadership Development: Growing as a Leader

Patrol Leadership

  • Take turns serving as patrol leader or assistant patrol leader
  • Serve as grubmaster to plan meals and manage cooking responsibilities
  • Act as patrol quartermaster to organize and maintain patrol equipment
  • Help plan patrol activities and menus
  • Lead by example in your patrol
  • Support newer Scouts as they join

Troop Roles

  • Volunteer for troop positions as you gain experience
  • Help with setup and cleanup at meetings and events
  • Assist with teaching skills to other Scouts
  • Participate actively in troop meetings and activities

Leadership Skills You’ll Develop

  • Communication and teamwork
  • Problem-solving and decision-making
  • Responsibility and accountability
  • Teaching and mentoring others (Scouting E.D.G.E. Method)
  • Planning and organization

Conferences and Reviews

  • Every rank requires a Scoutmaster Conference
  • Ranks beyond Scout Rank also require a Board of Review

Each rank advancement requires a board of review, where Scouts discuss their experiences and demonstrate their understanding of Scouting principles with adult leaders.

Merit Badge System

Merit badges are earned throughout your Scouting career and provide opportunities to explore interests and develop specialized skills. While not required for the First Class rank, merit badges complement your advancement journey.

Merit Badge Process

  • Talk to your Scoutmaster to get a blue card before starting any merit badge
  • Find counselors through our troop or the Lake Erie Council Merit Badge Counselor list
  • Work with merit badge counselors to complete requirements at your own pace
  • Complete requirements during troop meetings, camp, or individual study

Merit Badge Opportunities

  • Troop meetings and activities
  • Merit badge workshops at camps
  • Individual study with merit badge counselors
  • Community programs and classes
  • Merit badge universities
  • Summer camp programs

Merit Badge Documentation

  • Electronic Records: We use Scoutbook.com requirements tracking.
  • Merit Badge Blue Cards: Get one from your Scoutmaster before starting a new merit badge. Have three parts when used: one for troop, one for counselor, one for Scout.
  • Merit Badge Books: Optional but helpful resources (now available online!)

Important for First Class Scouts: While merit badges aren’t required for Scout through First Class ranks, earning merit badges during this period helps you explore interests and prepares you for Star rank, which requires 6 merit badges.

First Class Merit Badge Recommendations

Focus on merit badges that complement your rank advancement and can be worked on in parallel with rank requirements:

  • Cooking: Complements Second Class cooking requirements and is useful on every campout
  • First Aid: Complements First Class first aid requirements and teaches life-saving skills
  • Swimming or Hiking: Complements First Class fitness requirements and supports outdoor adventures
  • Camping: Complements camping skills learned in all ranks and enhances your outdoor experience

Popular Choices for New Scouts

  • Communications: Learn about different ways people communicate
  • Family Life: Explore family relationships and responsibilities
  • Personal Fitness: Develop healthy lifestyle habits
  • Citizenship in the Community: Learn about your local community and civic responsibility

More information: Scouting America Merit Badges

The Path to Eagle

The Eagle Scout rank is Scouting’s highest achievement, representing years of growth, leadership, and service. After earning First Class, your Scouting journey shifts from learning basic skills to developing expertise, leadership, and making a lasting impact on your community.

The Journey: Star to Eagle

Star → Life → Eagle

  • Emphasis shifts to leadership, service, and personal growth
  • Merit badge requirements become more diverse and challenging
  • Leadership positions require greater responsibility and longer tenure
  • Community service hours increase significantly

Star Scout Requirements

Merit Badges: Earn 6 merit badges (4 must be from Eagle-required list) Leadership: Serve actively in a leadership position for 4 months Service: Complete 6 hours of community service Skills: Demonstrate Scout spirit and outdoor skills proficiency

Life Scout Requirements

Merit Badges: Earn 5 additional merit badges (3 must be from Eagle-required list) Leadership: Serve actively in a leadership position for 6 months Service: Complete 6 hours of community service Skills: Plan, develop, and lead a service project

Eagle Scout Requirements

Merit Badge Requirements

Total: 21 merit badges (including 13 specific Eagle-required badges)

Eagle-Required Merit Badges (All 13 Required):

  1. First Aid - Emergency response and medical skills
  2. Citizenship in the Community - Local civic engagement
  3. Citizenship in the Nation - National civic responsibility
  4. Citizenship in the World - Global awareness and responsibility
  5. Communication - Written and verbal communication skills
  6. Personal Fitness - Physical health and wellness
  7. Emergency Preparedness OR Lifesaving - Crisis response skills
  8. Environmental Science OR Sustainability - Environmental stewardship
  9. Personal Management - Financial literacy and life skills
  10. Swimming OR Hiking OR Cycling - Physical fitness activity
  11. Camping - Advanced outdoor skills
  12. Cooking - Food preparation and nutrition
  13. Family Life - Relationship and family skills

Additional Merit Badges: 8 merit badges of your choice from any available options

Leadership Requirement

Position: Serve actively in a leadership position for 6 months, including:

  • Senior Patrol Leader
  • Assistant Senior Patrol Leader
  • Patrol Leader
  • Troop Guide
  • Order of the Arrow Representative
  • Den Chief
  • Scribe, Librarian, Historian, Quartermaster
  • Junior Assistant Scoutmaster
  • Chaplain Aide
  • Instructor
  • Webmaster
  • Leave No Trace Trainer
  • Outdoor Ethics Guide

Service Requirements

Community Service: Complete 6 hours of community service Eagle Scout Service Project: Plan, develop, and lead a service project that benefits a religious institution, school, or community organization (not Scouting)

The Eagle Scout Service Project

The Eagle project is the capstone of your Scouting experience and what often distinguishes Eagle Scouts as leaders throughout their lives.

Project Requirements

  • Benefits: Must help a religious institution, school, or community organization (not a Scouting unit)
  • Leadership: You must lead the project - others can help, but you’re in charge
  • Planning: Develop detailed project proposal, timeline, and budget
  • Approval: Get approval from your troop, beneficiary, and council before starting
  • Documentation: Keep detailed records of planning, execution, and results

Project Process

  1. Identify a Need: Work with organizations to find meaningful projects
  2. Develop Your Proposal: Create detailed plans, timelines, and resource requirements
  3. Get Approvals: Present your proposal to troop committee, beneficiary, and council
  4. Execute Your Project: Lead volunteers to complete the work
  5. Document Results: Complete final reports and documentation
  6. Present to Board of Review: Demonstrate your leadership and project impact

Project Ideas

  • Build outdoor classrooms or gardens for schools
  • Create accessibility improvements for community centers
  • Develop educational trails or outdoor learning spaces
  • Construct storage solutions for food banks or shelters
  • Organize community resource fairs or educational programs
  • Design and build playground equipment or outdoor fitness areas

Leadership Skills Developed

  • Project Management: Planning, budgeting, scheduling, and execution
  • Team Leadership: Motivating and directing volunteers
  • Communication: Presenting ideas, writing proposals, and coordinating with organizations
  • Problem Solving: Adapting to challenges and finding creative solutions
  • Service Leadership: Understanding community needs and creating lasting impact

Beyond Eagle: Continuing Your Journey

Palms and Continued Recognition

  • Bronze Palm: Earn 5 additional merit badges after Eagle
  • Gold Palm: Earn 5 more merit badges (10 total after Eagle)
  • Silver Palm: Earn 5 more merit badges (15 total after Eagle)
  • Continue earning palms throughout your Scouting career

Advanced Scouting Programs

  • Venturing: High adventure and specialized interests for ages 14-21
  • Sea Scouts: Maritime adventures and skills for ages 14-21
  • Order of the Arrow: Scouting’s national honor society

College and Career Benefits

  • Scholarships: Many Eagle Scout-specific scholarship opportunities
  • College Applications: Eagle Scout recognition enhances applications
  • Career Advantages: Leadership experience valued by employers
  • Networking: Lifelong connections with fellow Eagle Scouts
  • Life Skills: Project management, leadership, and service experience

Making Eagle Achievable

Start Early

  • Age 14: Begin working on Eagle-required merit badges
  • Age 15-16: Focus on leadership positions and advanced merit badges
  • Age 17: Plan and execute Eagle project, complete final requirements
  • Age 18: Final deadline - all requirements must be completed before 18th birthday

Stay Organized

  • Track Progress: Use Scoutbook to monitor advancement
  • Plan Merit Badges: Work on Eagle-required badges early
  • Document Service: Keep records of all service hours
  • Seek Mentorship: Work with Eagle Scouts and adult leaders

The Eagle Scout Legacy

Eagle Scout is more than a rank - it’s a commitment to lifelong leadership and service. Eagle Scouts are found among leaders in every field: business, government, military, education, and community service.

Remember: Only about 4% of all Scouts earn Eagle Scout. It requires dedication, persistence, and commitment - but the rewards last a lifetime.

The advancement path is clearly outlined in the Scout Handbook. Advancement is earned by each scout - it is not automatic and requires active participation and skill demonstration.

Advancement Path

The path to Eagle Scout consists of the following ranks in order:

  1. Scout
  2. Tenderfoot
  3. Second Class
  4. First Class
  5. Star
  6. Life
  7. Eagle

Key Principles

Individual Achievement

  • Scouts can work on requirements for any rank or merit badge at any time
  • Advancement is only awarded when ALL requirements for that rank are completed
  • Each scout progresses at their own pace

Requirement Sign-offs

  • Initially, girls get rank requirements signed off by more advanced scouts
  • Parents may NOT sign off on requirements (unlike Cub Scouts)
  • Scout Handbook must be brought to meetings and outings for sign-offs
  • Scout’s name MUST be inside and outside their handbook

Documentation and Records

Scout Handbook

  • Primary record of advancement progress
  • Check lists for rank requirements included
  • Corresponding pages provide learning information
  • Photocopy or photograph pages every 2-3 months in case of loss

Important Safety Net

Make regular copies of advancement pages because unlike Cub Scouts, if a handbook is lost, the advancement record is lost too.

Advancement Process

For Scout through First Class

  1. Work on requirements with more advanced scouts
  2. Complete all requirements for the rank
  3. Schedule Scoutmaster Conference
  4. Complete Board of Review (for ranks beyond Scout)

Required Components

  • Scoutmaster Conference: Required for every rank
  • Board of Review: Required for all ranks beyond Scout rank

Eagle Scout Requirements

The path to Eagle Scout requires:

  • Completion of all previous ranks
  • Earning required merit badges (21 total, including 14 specific required badges)
  • Demonstration of leadership through positions of responsibility
  • Completion of an Eagle Scout service project
  • Eagle Scout Board of Review

Tips for Success

Stay Organized

  • Keep Scout Handbook in good condition
  • Regularly update advancement records
  • Communicate with troop advancement coordinator

Be Proactive

  • Don’t wait for others to teach you - seek out learning opportunities
  • Practice skills regularly to maintain proficiency
  • Help younger scouts as you advance (leadership development)

Ask for Help

  • Advanced scouts and adult leaders are available to help
  • Don’t hesitate to ask questions about requirements
  • Attend merit badge workshops and special events
